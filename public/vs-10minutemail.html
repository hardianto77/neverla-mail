10 Minute Mail alternative: Neverla Mail vs 10minutemail

10 Minute Mail has done one thing since 2006: give you a private address that destroys itself ten minutes later. Neverla Mail gives you any address you type, keeps its mail five days, and reads it over a keyless API. The two are not rivals so much as opposite answers to “how long should this last?”. Facts read on 10minutemail.com’s own pages on 2 September 2026, with the section on where the ten-minute answer is the right one.

All comparisons

In short

  • Ten minutes versus five days. 10 Minute Mail deletes the address and everything in it after ten minutes, extendable ten at a time. Neverla Mail keeps every message 5 days. Neither is wrong; they are for different afternoons.
  • Private versus public. 10 Minute Mail’s address is unique to you and nobody else can open it. Neverla Mail’s mailboxes are public — anyone who knows the address can read them — and each has an alias to hand out instead. On this row 10 Minute Mail wins outright.
  • A person versus a program. 10 Minute Mail has no API, no app and no extension: a browser tab with a countdown. Neverla Mail has a keyless REST API, an MCP server for agents and your own domain for free.
  • Pick 10 Minute Mail for a one-off sign-up where you want the address gone before you finish your coffee. Pick Neverla Mail for an address you chose, mail that outlives the hour, and anything a script or an agent reads.

What 10 Minute Mail does well

There is a reason the name became a generic term. Four things 10 Minute Mail does that Neverla Mail does not:

  • The inbox is yours alone. The address is unique and nobody else can open it — no password, no account, just an address nobody can guess. Neverla Mail’s public mailboxes are the opposite by design, and the alias is the workaround.
  • Nothing touches a disk. Its security page says all data lives in memory, is never written to storage, and has no retention period beyond the session. That is a stronger privacy claim than Neverla Mail makes: here mail sits on a disk for 5 days.
  • It replies. Some sites require an answer to a message before they let you in; 10 Minute Mail lets you reply and forward from the page. Neverla Mail is receive-only, by design, and cannot.
  • It is simpler than anything. One page, one address, one timer, since November 2006, in 47 languages. There is no setting to get wrong.

Where Neverla Mail is different

Neverla Mail is built for the case a ten-minute inbox cannot serve: mail that has to be there later, addresses you chose, and readers that are not people.

  • 5 days. A trial that emails on day three, a receipt you want tomorrow, a code you read after a meeting — all gone at 10 Minute Mail, all still here. Retention is enforced by a job, not by a countdown you have to keep clicking.
  • Any address, on 3 domains or your own. Type trial@mail.neverla.web.id and it exists; point a domain at smtp.mail.neverla.web.id and every address on it exists. 10 Minute Mail assigns you one address and offers no domain of your own.
  • An API and an MCP server. A test suite polls /api/v1/mailbox with no key; an agent opens an inbox over MCP and waits up to 25 seconds for a message. 10 Minute Mail has neither and never claimed to.
  • No ads. 10 Minute Mail runs display advertising to stay free. Neverla Mail runs none, has no analytics and no third-party script, and stays free by selling an off-blocklist domain pool to developers.

For developers: reading a mailbox

10 Minute Mail publishes no API, so this section is one-sided. The command below reads a Neverla Mail mailbox with no header, no key and no account:

shell
$ curl -sG https://mail.neverla.web.id/api/v1/mailbox --data-urlencode "address=anything@mail.neverla.web.id" | jq '{count, alias}'

A 200 with count: 0 and an alias is the whole proof. The reference is three endpoints — list a mailbox, read a message, delete it — and the Python and Node.js guides show a wait loop with a deadline for a test that expects a code.

If you have been driving 10 Minute Mail with a browser automation to make it act as an API, stop: it is against the grain of a page built for a person, and a keyless GET does the same job in one line.

For AI agents

An agent cannot use 10 Minute Mail without steering a browser, reading a countdown and hoping the tab survives. Neverla Mail’s server at https://mail.neverla.web.id/mcp gives it an inbox with no key: create_inbox, a blocking wait_for_message of up to 25 seconds, read_message.

Email for AI agents covers the four-call loop and the guard rails; the client guide has the configuration for Claude, Cursor and five others.

Switching from 10 Minute Mail

There is nothing to migrate — that is the point of a ten-minute inbox — so the switch is a habit:

  1. Type a name instead of copying one. On the front page, choose any local part on mail.neverla.web.id, mixozia.com or another of the 3 public domains. It is an inbox from the first message and stays one; there is no timer to extend.
  2. Hand out the alias, not the address. Every Neverla Mail mailbox has an alias that delivers to it and cannot be used to open it. Give the alias to the form; keep the address. That is how a public mailbox gets most of the privacy 10 Minute Mail gives you for free.
  3. Delete when you are done, if you want to. A message can be deleted from the inbox or the API at once; otherwise it goes after 5 days. If you liked that 10 Minute Mail forgot you automatically, this is the button to press.
  4. Keep 10 Minute Mail for the one-offs. There is no reason to have only one throwaway habit. A private ten-minute address for a download gate; a chosen five-day address for a trial.

Which one, for what

The honest split is by how long the mail has to live and who reads it:

Pick Neverla Mail when

  • the message may arrive later than ten minutes from now, or you want it tomorrow;
  • you want to choose the address, reuse it, or use your own domain;
  • a script, a test or an agent reads the mail.

Pick 10 Minute Mail when

  • you want the address gone the moment you are done, with no button to press;
  • the inbox must be readable by you alone, and nothing may touch a disk;
  • the site you are signing up to requires a reply.

When to stay with 10 Minute Mail

Three situations where switching would cost you something real:

  • Privacy is the whole reason. A unique address only you can open, held in memory, gone in ten minutes: that is a better privacy story than a public mailbox on a disk for 5 days, and no alias changes it.
  • You need to reply. Neverla Mail cannot send a single message, to anyone, ever. If the form wants an answer by mail, 10 Minute Mail can give one.
  • You like the countdown. Forgetting is a feature. If you would rather never see the inbox again than have to delete it, the timer is doing you a service.

Questions

Is Neverla Mail a 10 Minute Mail alternative?

For a throwaway address, yes, with two differences that matter: Neverla Mail keeps mail 5 days and lets you choose the address, and its mailboxes are public where 10 Minute Mail’s are private. It also has an API and an MCP server, which 10 Minute Mail does not.

Does 10 Minute Mail have an API?

No API, SDK, app or extension is published on its site as of 2 September 2026. Neverla Mail’s API is three keyless endpoints, documented in the reference.

Can I keep a 10 Minute Mail address longer?

Ten minutes at a time, by clicking “Give me 10 more minutes”; after it expires the mail is deleted and the address stops working. A Neverla Mail address needs no extending, and its mail lasts 5 days.

Which one is more private?

10 Minute Mail, clearly: a unique address only you can see, stored in memory only. Every Neverla Mail mailbox can be read by anyone who knows the address; the alias limits who does. Never use either for anything you would mind a stranger reading.

Can I use my own domain with either?

Only with Neverla Mail: one MX record at smtp.mail.neverla.web.id turns any domain you own into a catch-all inbox, free. 10 Minute Mail offers no domain feature. The catch-all guide shows the setup.
