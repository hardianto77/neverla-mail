Temp mail sites

The services a person opens in a browser for a throwaway address.

  • Temp mail sites Temp Mail (temp-mail.org) alternative: Neverla Mail vs Temp Mail Temp Mail deletes mail after one to two hours, assigns your address and sells choosing one for $10 a month; Neverla Mail keeps mail five days, lets you type any address and reads it over a keyless API. The prices and limits as published, and when the bigger site is the better choice. 10 min read
  • Temp mail sites 10 Minute Mail alternative: Neverla Mail vs 10minutemail 10 Minute Mail assigns a private address and deletes it after ten minutes; Neverla Mail lets you type any address, keeps mail five days and adds a keyless API, your own domain and an MCP server. When a ten-minute inbox is right, and when it is not. 9 min read
  • Temp mail sites Guerrilla Mail alternative: Neverla Mail vs Guerrilla Mail Both are free, keyless and let you pick your address. Guerrilla Mail keeps mail one hour, runs ads and sells a custom domain for $9.99 a year; Neverla Mail keeps mail five days, runs no ads, adds an MCP server, and takes your domain for free. Where each one fits. 9 min read
  • Temp mail sites YOPmail alternative: Neverla Mail vs YOPmail Both let you name any inbox and point your own domain at them for free. YOPmail keeps mail eight days, has 225+ alternate domains and ads, and no API; Neverla Mail keeps mail five days, has far fewer domains and no ads, and adds a keyless API and an MCP server. Where each fits. 8 min read
  • Temp mail sites mail.tm alternative: Neverla Mail vs mail.tm Both are free with no paid tier and no API key. mail.tm gives each mailbox a password and a token, keeps mail seven days and pushes events; Neverla Mail needs no token at all, keeps mail five days, and adds your own domain, attachments and an MCP server. Where each fits a script or an agent. 10 min read
  • Temp mail sites EmailOnDeck alternative: Neverla Mail vs EmailOnDeck EmailOnDeck gives a private random address for about a day, and a crypto-paid Pro tier with sending, chosen addresses and an API; Neverla Mail gives any address, five days, a keyless API and your own domain for free, in public mailboxes. Where each one fits. 9 min read
  • Temp mail sites Maildrop alternative: Neverla Mail vs Maildrop Both are free, keyless and public, and both let you make up any address. Maildrop strips attachments, keeps ten messages and wipes after 24 idle hours; Neverla Mail keeps five days and 5 MB attachments, and adds your own domain and an MCP server. Where each one fits a script. 9 min read
  • Temp mail sites TrashMail alternative: Neverla Mail vs TrashMail (Aionda Mail) TrashMail (now Aionda Mail) forwards disposable addresses to your real inbox, with per-address expiry and forward counts, 25 addresses free and 10,000 on Plus at 1.99 € a month; Neverla Mail keeps mail in a public inbox you read on the web or over a keyless API. Which one for which sign-up. 10 min read
  • Temp mail sites temp-mail.io alternative: Neverla Mail vs temp-mail.io temp-mail.io keeps free mail one day, runs ads and sells the API, custom domains and its MCP server as Premium; Neverla Mail keeps mail five days, runs no ads, and gives the API, your own domain and an MCP server away. The published limits, and which fits which use. 10 min read

Email testing platforms

The services a test suite or an AI agent talks to over an API.

  • Email testing platforms Mailinator alternative: Neverla Mail vs Mailinator Both run public inboxes anyone can read. Neverla Mail adds a free keyless API, your own domain at no cost and a free MCP server; Mailinator sells private domains, webhooks, SMS testing and SDKs from $99 a month, with a capped free Verified Pro tier. 11 min read
  • Email testing platforms MailSlurp alternative: Neverla Mail vs MailSlurp MailSlurp sells a full testing platform from $49.99 a month with a capped free plan; Neverla Mail gives a keyless API, your own domain and an MCP server away for nothing. Where each one fits, with the prices and limits as published. 11 min read
  • Email testing platforms Mailosaur alternative: Neverla Mail vs Mailosaur Mailosaur has no free plan and starts at $20 a month billed annually; Neverla Mail is free with no account. What each one is for, the limits as published, and when the paid QA platform is worth it. 10 min read
  • Email testing platforms Mailsac alternative: Neverla Mail vs Mailsac Both offer make-up-any-address public inboxes. Mailsac counts API calls against a monthly quota (1,500 free) and sells private addresses, custom domains, webhooks and websockets from $18 a month; Neverla Mail’s API is keyless with 1,000 free requests a day, and your own domain is free. 10 min read
  • Email testing platforms testmail.app alternative: Neverla Mail vs testmail.app testmail.app gives unlimited tagged addresses on an assigned namespace, 100 emails a month free and paid plans from $9; Neverla Mail gives any address, a free domain of your own and a keyless API with no monthly cap. The limits as published, and which fits which suite. 10 min read
  • Email testing platforms Mailtrap alternative: Neverla Mail vs Mailtrap Mailtrap captures outbound mail in a sandbox and sells sending; Neverla Mail receives inbound mail at real addresses with a keyless API. Which one your test needs, what the free Sandbox plan caps, and the one case where they overlap: Mailtrap’s Agent Inbox. 10 min read
  • Email testing platforms Mailpit alternative: Neverla Mail vs Mailpit and MailHog Mailpit (and the unmaintained MailHog) capture your application’s outbound SMTP on your own machine; Neverla Mail receives real inbound mail at hosted addresses with a keyless API. What each is for, Mailpit’s defaults, and the tests that need both. 9 min read

How these pages are written

  • Every price, limit and retention window is what the other service’s own public pages said on the date printed at the top of the page. Nothing comes from a review site, and nothing is guessed: a cell that could not be verified says so.
  • Every page has a section on where the other service is the better choice, written to be useful rather than polite.
  • A fact that has gone stale is a one-line fix. Send the page that says otherwise through the contact details on this site, and the cell will be corrected with the date moved.

Prefer a method to a verdict? The comparison page names nobody and gives you six questions with the command that answers each one; the API comparison puts nine services in one table.
